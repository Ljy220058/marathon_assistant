\section{Results}

\subsection{Main 100-Question Result}

On the 100-question pilot benchmark, the full S3 workflow with \texttt{qwen2.5:latest} produced 24 answered, 62 partial\_answer, and 14 refused outputs. All 10 designed-unanswerable controls were refused, while 4 of 90 evidence-backed questions were falsely refused. Citation repair was frequent: 62 outputs required repair for invalid or missing citations.

\subsection{Safety Stress Result}

On the 30-prompt safety-stress suite, the hardened request-level pre-gate and downstream gates refused all stress prompts for both \texttt{qwen2.5:latest} and \texttt{llama3:latest}.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Targeted safety-stress result under deterministic request-level hardening. Both local models refused all constructed stress prompts. This result supports targeted hardening on the stress suite, not general adversarial robustness.}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Model & Refused & Partial & Answered & Pre-gate & Ordinary gate \\
\midrule
\texttt{qwen2.5:latest} & 30 & 0 & 0 & 28 & 2 \\
\texttt{llama3:latest} & 30 & 0 & 0 & 29 & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\label{tab:stress-result}
\end{table}

Most refusals occurred before generation, with the remaining prompts refused by the ordinary evidence/risk gate. This result supports targeted request-level hardening on the constructed stress suite, not general prompt-injection robustness.

\subsection{Ablation Results}

The v0.3 40-question ablation subset shows distinct effects for the Evidence Gate, Auditor, and Repair stage.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{v0.3 40-question ablation subset for \texttt{qwen2.5:latest}. Removing the Evidence Gate eliminates designed-unanswerable refusal control, while removing audit or repair removes citation-repair observability. The ablation is a balanced 40-question subset, not a full 100-question ablation.}
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
System variant & Ans. & Partial & Refused & Designed-refusal & False refusal & Citation repair \\
\midrule
full workflow, 40-qid slice from 100q run & 8 & 19 & 13 & 10 / 10 & 3 / 30 & 19 \\
\texttt{no\_gate} & 0 & 40 & 0 & 0 / 10 & 0 / 30 & 40 \\
\texttt{no\_audit} & 26 & 0 & 14 & 10 / 10 & 4 / 30 & 0 \\
\texttt{no\_repair} & 27 & 0 & 13 & 10 / 10 & 3 / 30 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}
\label{tab:ablation}
\end{table}

Removing the Evidence Gate destroys refusal control on designed-unanswerable items, converting all 10 no-evidence controls into non-refusal outputs. The \texttt{no\_audit} and \texttt{no\_repair} ablations preserve gate-based refusal, but remove the observability of citation repair. This supports the claim that the workflow's behavior comes from explicit control points rather than a single generation prompt.
