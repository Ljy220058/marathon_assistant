\subsection{Rule Facts and Predicates}

M-EXRx records the binding decision in the rule layer. Each rule is represented as
\[
r=\langle id, layer, priority, body, decision, obligations, prohibitions,
evidence, trace\rangle .
\]
The body uses a RuleML-style implication shape, and the consequent emits
decision, obligation, prohibition, and trace atoms. The decision vocabulary is
\texttt{allow}, \texttt{downgrade}, \texttt{ask\_clarification},
\texttt{repair\_required}, and \texttt{refuse}.
An output is valid only when its status, evidence identifiers, action
identifiers, prohibitions, trace fields, and highest-priority fired rule are
mutually consistent.
Decision atoms are mapped to benchmark statuses as follows:
\texttt{allow} emits \texttt{answered}; \texttt{downgrade} or
\texttt{repair\_required} emits \texttt{partial\_answer};
\texttt{ask\_clarification} emits \texttt{ask\_clarification}; and
\texttt{refuse} emits \texttt{refused}.

\subsection{Priority and Conflict Resolution}

Let \(\mathcal{R}\) be the rule set and \(>\subseteq \mathcal{R}\times
\mathcal{R}\) be a strict priority relation. If \(r_i > r_j\) and the
consequents of \(r_i\) and \(r_j\) conflict, the consequent of \(r_i\) is
selected. The rule-level relation is induced by the layer order below:
\(r_i > r_j\) when \(layer(r_i)\) outranks \(layer(r_j)\). If no applicable
priority relation resolves the conflict, unresolved safety or prohibition
conflicts fail closed to \texttt{refused}, while missing or contradictory
profile information fails closed to \texttt{ask\_clarification}.

The domain priority order is:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
red\_flags > injury/fatigue/environment > scope > evidence\\
> protocol > capacity > progression > user\_preference .
\end{array}
\]
Thus, a preference for intervals cannot override chest pain, heat illness,
acute injury escalation, or missing prescription evidence.

Any valid solver output must expose the same permission objects: selected
status, applicable evidence identifiers, permitted action identifiers, fired
rules, and required trace fields. Evidence eligibility is narrower than
retrieval relevance: protocol rules and active action-library entries may
authorize prescription, while ordinary retrieved literature remains
explanation-only unless promoted into an approved rule or action.
